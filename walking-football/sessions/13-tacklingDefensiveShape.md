# Tackling and Defensive Shape

## Session Aim

Teach players to defend effectively without chasing opponents all over the pitch.

By the end of the session, players should understand:

- **From behind:** stop the turn and force the ball back.
- **From the front:** close, slow, show one way.
- **In team defence:** mark the player in your zone, then pass them on.
- **When possession is lost:** recover shape before chasing the ball.

**Session length:** 60 minutes

---

## 1. Warm-Up – Defensive Movement

**Duration:** 10 minutes

Use an area roughly 15 x 15 metres.

Players work in pairs, with one player leading and one defending.

Start without a ball:

- The attacker walks freely around the area.
- The defender mirrors them while staying roughly 1–2 metres away.
- The defender tries to remain between the attacker and a designated end line.

Progress by giving the attacker a ball.

The defender still does not tackle. Their job is to stay balanced, move their feet and block the dangerous route.

Finish with short 1v1 situations where the attacker tries to cross an end line.

### Coaching Points

- Stay balanced.
- Do not get square.
- Do not dive in.
- Move your feet.
- Block the dangerous route.

---

## 2. No Turn Drill

**Duration:** 10 minutes

Set up a small area approximately 8 x 10 metres.

The attacker starts with their back to goal and the defender behind them.

A support player stands behind or to the side of the attacker.

The attacker tries to turn and progress towards the goal or end line.

The defender's objective is not necessarily to win the ball.

The defender scores if they:

- Prevent the attacker turning.
- Force a sideways pass.
- Force a backward pass.

The attacker scores if they turn successfully and progress forward.

Rotate players regularly.

### Main Coaching Cue

> **Behind them? Don't tackle through them — stop the turn.**

The defender should stay close enough to influence the attacker without barging, pushing or reaching around them.

---

## 3. Front-On Defending

**Duration:** 10 minutes

Create a channel approximately 6 metres wide and 10 metres long.

The attacker starts at one end with the ball.

The defender starts around 4 metres away.

The attacker tries to reach the opposite end.

The defender practises:

**Close → Slow → Angle → Contain**

The defender should encourage the attacker towards one side rather than approaching square-on and allowing them to choose either direction.

Initially, the defender does not need to win the ball.

Award the defender a point for:

- Forcing the attacker backwards.
- Forcing the attacker out of the channel.
- Preventing forward progress.

Then allow tackles when the attacker exposes the ball.

### Main Coaching Cue

> **Don't rush to win it. Make them go where you want them to go.**

---

## 4. Zonal Man-for-Man Defending

**Duration:** 15 minutes

This is the main tactical part of the session.

Use approximately half a pitch and divide the width into three channels:

```text
LEFT              CENTRE              RIGHT

|                  |                  |
|                  |                  |
|                  |                  |
|                  |                  |
```

Play 3v3 initially.

Each defender takes responsibility for attackers entering their channel.

### Main Rule

> **You can follow your player inside your zone. When they leave, hand them over.**

Attackers should deliberately interchange positions.

Defenders must communicate.

Useful calls include:

- **"Yours!"**
- **"I've got them!"**
- **"Passing!"**

### Important Principle

> **Do not release your player until the next defender acknowledges them.**

Stop play occasionally when an attacker becomes free and ask:

**"Who had responsibility for that player?"**

This reinforces the handover principle without encouraging defenders to chase opponents all over the pitch.

---

## 5. Recover Into Shape Game

**Duration:** 10 minutes

Remove the visible channels and play a small-sided game.

Introduce one defensive rule:

> **When possession is lost, the defending team must recover behind halfway before aggressively pressing.**

Once the team is organised, they defend using the zonal man-for-man principles.

Encourage players to:

**Protect the middle → Identify attackers → Pressure locally → Pass players on**

Avoid stopping the game too often.

Look particularly for situations where a defender follows an opponent unnecessarily and leaves a large gap.

Freeze play occasionally to show players the space that has appeared.

---

## 6. Final Game

**Duration:** 5 minutes

Play normal walking football with no artificial restrictions.

Before starting, give the team three defensive reminders:

> **Behind — stop the turn.**

> **In front — show them away from danger.**

> **Team — hold your shape and pass players on.**

Observe whether these behaviours now appear naturally in the game.

---

## Session Message

Do not judge defenders only by how many tackles they make.

A successful defensive sequence might be:

**Forward pass prevented → attacker cannot turn → ball goes sideways → ball goes backwards**

The key lesson is:

> **You don't have to win the ball to win the defensive battle.**

---

## Suggested Follow-Up Session

The natural progression is **two-defender cooperation**:

- One defender pressures.
- The second defender covers.
- Defenders learn when to switch roles.
- The pair work together to protect central areas and prevent forward progression.

This links individual defending directly into the team's zonal defensive shape.
